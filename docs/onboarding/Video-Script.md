# Onboarding Video Script

A five-minute narrated walkthrough, with screen directions and timing cues for recording

Target runtime 5:00 to 5:30 · Record at 1920x1080 · Version 1.0

## Before You Record

- Sign in to a workspace with a full policy dataset loaded and at least one saved conversation with a strong cited answer, plus one pinned answer.
- Close other tabs, silence notifications, and set the browser to a clean 1920x1080 window.
- Read the narration once aloud before recording; the script is written for a conversational pace of roughly 140 words per minute.
- Pause one beat after each click so viewers' eyes can catch up.

Shot 1: Opening 0:00 to 0:25

**On screen**  
The sign-in page at policytoaction.beauhawked.com. Hold on the tagline for a beat before moving.

**Narration**  
"Welcome to Policy to Action. This is a decision-support platform built for school administrators. You describe a real situation in plain language, and it answers using only your district's own board policies and handbooks, with every claim cited back to the exact source text. Let me show you how it works, start to finish, in about five minutes."

Shot 2: Creating an account 0:25 to 0:55

**On screen**  
Click Create a workspace. Slowly point the cursor at each field: first name, last name, district, email, password. Do not submit on camera.

**Narration**  
"Getting started takes one form. Your name, your district, your work email, and a password. After you submit, the platform emails you a verification link; click it and you are in. One note before we go further: your district's data is private to your workspace, and everything you ask stays in your own account."

Shot 3: The question screen 0:55 to 1:30

**On screen**  
The signed-in home screen with the personalized greeting. Hover over the sources pill in the header, then over the three example questions.

**Narration**  
"Here is home base. The greeting is yours, and this pill in the corner shows exactly which policy set your answers will draw from; today that is our district's board policies, three hundred thirty-two of them. If you are not sure where to begin, these example questions are one click away. But the real power is describing your own situation, so let's do that."

Shot 4: Asking a scenario 1:30 to 2:10

**On screen**  
Type a realistic multi-part scenario into the composer, visibly using "Student A" and "Student B". Pause on the privacy note under the composer, then press Enter and let the answer begin rendering.

**Narration**  
"I am describing a real kind of situation: two students, an altercation, one with an IEP, and several decisions to make at once. Notice two things. First, I am using placeholders, Student A and Student B, never real names; the note under the box reminds you every time. Second, I can write as much detail as I need, because more specific scenarios get more precise citations. Press Enter, and the platform goes to work."

Shot 5: Reading the answer 2:10 to 3:00

**On screen**  
Scroll slowly through the answer: policy summaries, then the citation chips, then Recommended Actions, then Implications.

**Narration**  
"Every answer has the same structure. At the top, the specific policies that apply, each named with its code. Beneath them, citation chips, one for every source used. Then numbered recommended actions, in the order you would actually take them. And finally implications: what following, or not following, these policies means for students, staff, and the district. This is guidance shaped for a decision, not a wall of text."

Shot 6: Verifying the evidence 3:00 to 3:40

**On screen**  
Click a citation chip. Let the evidence panel open and hold on the quoted text. Click Open full source briefly, return, close the panel, then point at the Evidence button in the header.

**Narration**  
"Here is the habit that makes this platform defensible. Click any citation, and the evidence panel shows the exact policy language the answer was grounded in, word for word. One more click opens the complete policy. Before any consequential decision, take thirty seconds and read the source. And if you close the panel, this Evidence button brings it right back."

Shot 7: Pins and history 3:40 to 4:15

**On screen**  
Click Pinned under the answer, then open the Pinned view from the rail and show the saved card. Then open History and reopen a past conversation.

**Narration**  
"When an answer is worth keeping, pin it. Pinned answers become your personal reference library, each with its evidence frozen at pin time, and they are saved to your account, not your browser. Every conversation also lands in History automatically, so you can reopen last month's situation and pick up exactly where you left off."

Shot 8: The Library 4:15 to 4:50

**On screen**  
Open the Library. Type a term into search, open a policy, scroll to the Related policies chips, and click one.

**Narration**  
"The Library holds every policy in your district's manual, fully searchable. Open any policy to read the complete text, and at the bottom, related policies: the ones this policy references, and its neighbors in the same section. Policy questions rarely live alone, and neither do the answers."

Shot 9: Closing 4:50 to 5:20

**On screen**  
Return to a fresh question screen. Hold on the greeting, then fade or cut to a title card with the URL policytoaction.beauhawked.com.

**Narration**  
"That is the whole loop: describe the situation, read the structured guidance, verify the source, and keep what is worth keeping. Two reminders as you start. Use placeholders instead of student names. And remember this is decision support, not legal advice; the citations exist so you can verify before you act. Sign in at policytoaction.beauhawked.com, and ask it something real."

## Recording Notes

- Total narration is roughly 700 words, which lands near 5:10 at a conversational pace.
- If you produce audience-specific cuts, swap Shot 2 for a setup-focused segment (importing policies via Add source) for setup administrators, and trim Shot 2 entirely for the dissertation participant cut if accounts are pre-provisioned.
- Captions: export the narration column as your caption file; it is written to be read verbatim.
